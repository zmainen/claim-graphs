# Modules: a claim graph in two grains

**Status:** proposed
**Issue:** [#53](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/53)
**Frames:** [#37](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/37) · [#21](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/21) · [#17](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/17) · [#13](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/13)
**Depends on:** `docs/claim-format.md` (questions, `addresses`, roles, stance), `scripts/relations.py`, the `questions`, `stance`, `parts`, `prediction-outcome` and `warrant` layers, `schema/claim-set-v0.schema.json` (`views`)
**Shown on:** Gädeke 2026, claim-tree v7, at https://claude.ai/code/artifact/a0376de2-2844-404f-af20-7a97adeb67b7

The chain atomizes a paper well, and the result is seventy claims where a reader holds a dozen
things in mind. Many pieces of evidence point at one claim; all of them resolve one hypothesis
and its rivals; nothing names the cluster. This note says what the cluster is, separates what
it should look like from how it is found, and specifies a mechanical layer that finds it from
the edges the graph already has.

## What the structure is

**A module is one question under test.** It holds the question, the hypothesis the paper
commits to, the alternatives the paper raises in order to reject, the predictions the
hypothesis entails, the findings that test them or bear on the hypothesis directly, and the
interpretation the paper draws. That is the *argument grain*: a handful of nodes, the ones a
reader of the paper holds in mind. A module carries a warrant, which is its hypothesis's, and
a summary sentence, which for a question module the hypothesis already is.

**A finding is a module of its own, one level down.** Every empirical claim that reaches the
argument is a finding, and its details hang beneath it: the parts it is composed of, the
controls that validate it, the sub-results that support it, the caveats that qualify it or
stand in tension with it, the panel it sits in, its verification. That is the *finding grain*,
and it is where the atomization is absorbed. A finding's dozen numbers are its details, not the
paper's argument. Nothing at this grain is lost; nothing at this grain is on the page until it
is opened.

**The finding and its results are one cluster, and the cluster is the unit.** Call the head a
*finding* and what hangs beneath it *results*: the analysis results that bear on it. Results
are claim files in the format, because coverage and verification need atoms with spans and
panels; they are not claims on the paper, and nothing that shows or exports a module may call
them claims. The cluster has two operations, and both are why it exists. *Fold*: across
versions the cluster is the unit of identity. The finding carries its results, so a result
that appears or vanishes is a change inside the finding, not a new claim on the paper, and
the matcher should align findings and compare results beneath them. *Combine*: for strength
the cluster is the unit of warrant. A finding's level is a function of its results, what
validates it, what supports it, what qualifies it, whether its parts reproduced, which is what
the warrant rule's clauses already compute and the layer records explicitly as `combine`.
On a paper, the cluster is what is reported as one thing; the argument grain above it is a
different object and is not what a paper lists.

**Scope belongs to the module and has two halves.** The *domain* is what the claims apply to:
the sample, the task, the design, drawn from the scope claims the members are scoped by. The
*apparatus* is what they rest on: the models, the model selection, the GLM, the localizer,
drawn from what the members require. Scope claims stay claims, adjudicable and cited to a
span. But they are not members of the argument; they are its ground, and the module shows them
as one line that opens. This separates the two meanings of scope that #37 found written
identically: a scope *claim* is a proposition the paper asserts; a module's scope is a domain.

**An observation is a finding that answers no declared question.** A whole-brain result no
hypothesis predicted; a model that fitted best. It is a module of the second kind, with its
details, and it is what a later paper makes a hypothesis of. A paper that is mostly
observations is telling you something true about it. There is no third kind: apparatus is
scope, and anything else is a missing edge.

**What it changes.** The paper page reads as its questions; under each, the argument grain is
visible at once and the finding grain opens on demand. Adjudication gains a unit above the
claim: is this the paper's question, is the hypothesis stated right, is the scope right, then
claim by claim. Warrant is reported where it is meant, the hypothesis's from its predictions
and rivals, with the findings' warrants beneath. Exports carry the module where they can: the
claim-set schema's `views` directly, DocMaps as a structured unit, MIRA in the extended
namespace. Coverage and verification are untouched, because they work on the atoms and should.
It is the natural unit for per-module review, publication and funding.

## How it is found

The structure is derived from the edges and nothing else. Membership is mechanical; the only
model call is a summary sentence where the hypothesis cannot serve.

1. **Seed.** Every question in the paper's `questions:` opens a module. The hypotheses and
   alternatives that `addresses` it are its core; an alternative is a hypothesis with stance
   `rejects` or `entertains`.
2. **Argument grain.** From the core, outward over argument edges, breadth-first so the nearest
   core wins: a prediction joins by `entails` from a hypothesis; a finding joins by `tests`,
   `confirms`, `refutes`, `supports` or `extends` aimed at a hypothesis or prediction, or by
   `rules-out` aimed at an alternative; an interpretation or synthesis joins by `interprets`
   or `supports` aimed at a member; a claim joins by `in-tension-with` or `dissociates-with` a
   member, since a contrast or tension is between claims of one argument.
3. **Finding grain.** A claim not yet placed that reaches a placed finding, prediction,
   hypothesis or interpretation by `part-of`, `validates`, `supports`, `extends`, `qualifies`
   or `in-tension-with` is a detail of that node and joins its module. Repeat to a fixed
   point, one level at a time, so a detail of a detail is placed too.
4. **Scope.** For every member, what it `requires` or is `enables-method` by is the module's
   apparatus; what `scopes` it is the module's domain. A claim that hangs off an apparatus or
   domain claim by a detail edge hangs there.
5. **Observations.** A claim still unplaced whose role is empirical or control and which is
   the whole of something (no outgoing detail edge) opens an observation module; step 3 runs
   again for its details.
6. **Loose.** Whatever remains is loose. Loose is a lint, not a category: it names a missing
   edge, and the layer lists each with what it would take to place it.

On Gädeke v7 this places 65 of 68 claims into three question modules (53 claims), three
observations and seven scope claims, once three edges a reading would add are present, and
it leaves three apparatus claims loose, each wanting one `requires` edge. Without those three
edges the derivation leaves two syntheses and one caveat unplaced, which is the finding that
motivates the two rules below.

## Two rules for the edge task

The gaps the derivation found are not the derivation's. They are two things the edge-inference
task never asks for, and both become instructions in the task file and rules in the linter:

- **A synthesis or interpretation carries an argument edge outward.** The task wrote edges
  into Gädeke's syntheses from their details and none out of them, so the risk-preference
  synthesis that eliminates the risk-attitude alternative pointed at nothing. A synthesis with
  only incoming edges is loose.
- **A result the reader declines to confirm still gets the edge that says what it bears on.**
  The uncorrected IFG cluster was rightly not written as confirming the connectivity
  prediction, and was then written as nothing. `qualifies` is the edge for a result that
  bounds a finding without supporting it.

## The layer

`modules`: kind feature, scope paper, mechanical, needs `claim-tree`, `questions`, `stance`
and `prediction-outcome`, reads `scripts/modules.py`, produces `runs/<paper>/modules.json`
and `views/<paper>.modules.json`: one object per module with `id`, `kind`
(`question` | `observation`), `question`, `hypotheses`, `alternatives` with what eliminated
each, `predictions` with outcome and the findings that test them, `findings` as clusters, each with its `results` and a `combine` record naming the
results its warrant was computed from, `interpretations`, `domain`, `apparatus`, `warrant`, `summary`, and
the member list; plus `loose` with a reason per claim. It is idempotent and byte-stable, so it
can run on every tree without a prompt. A second, optional step, `module-summaries`, is the
one prompt per paper: a sentence per observation module and per question module whose
hypothesis exceeds a length, answered as `synthesis` is today.

The site reads `views/<paper>.modules.json`: the paper page leads with modules, the argument
grain open, the finding grain folded; the claim page names its module. The claim-set export
carries modules in `views`. Adjudication's skeleton gains a verdict per module (`question`,
`hypothesis`, `scope`) ahead of the per-claim verdicts.

## What this does not decide

Whether a claim may belong to two modules. The derivation gives each claim one owner, the
nearest core; a finding that tests predictions of two hypotheses is placed with the first and
listed on the second. Whether that is right is a question the Gädeke reading can answer.

Whether modules cross papers. The same hypothesis addressed in two papers is the registry's
question (#13); a module is per paper until then.

The structured domain, organism, task, population, measurement, as fields rather than a
list of scope claims. It is a reading of the scope claims and can be added to the summaries
step when it is wanted.

## Cost

The layer with tests: one day. The two edge-task rules and the linter: two hours. The site's
module view: one day. The summaries step: half a day. Running the layer on every tree:
minutes; on Gädeke, one edge-inference re-answer to test the two rules.

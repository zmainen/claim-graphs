# The canon: one declaration of every concept, versioned, rendered everywhere

**Status:** proposed
**Issue:** [#63](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/63)
**Frames:** [#53](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/53) · [#21](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/21) · [#20](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/20) · [#9](https://github.com/zmainen/claim-graphs/issues/9)
**Depends on:** `scripts/relations.py`, `extract/claim_graphs/vocabulary.py`, `extract/claim_graphs/contract.py`, `extract/claim_graphs/skill.py`, `pipeline/layers.yaml`, `scripts/pipeline.py` (declaration versions and `approve --declaration`), `docs/design/2026-09-12-kinds-of-decision.md`

The machinery already renders its relations and roles from one declaration into the prompts and
the skill, and checks that neither drifts. That is the right shape, and it covers half of what
the system means. The other half, question, finding cluster, module, scope, observation,
warrant, check, decision kind, procedure, lives in design notes, which are rationale and
history, not a reference. And the examples that make the declarations concrete are quotations
from one corpus, so nothing renders or checks without a corpus checkout. This note widens the
existing mechanism to every concept, gives it a version that runs record, replaces corpus
quotations with a toy paper, and renders the whole as a page.

## What the canon is

One declaration set, `canon/`, holding every concept at every grain: the atom (claim,
assertion, result), the relations, the roles and stance, the question, the finding cluster
with fold and combine, the module, scope as domain and apparatus, observation, warrant, the
checks, the four kinds of decision, the adjudication procedure. Every entry has the same
fields:

| Field | What it holds |
|:--|:--|
| `definition` | one paragraph, the thing itself |
| `signals` | what a reader uses to recognise it in a paper |
| `distinct-from` | pairs, with the sentence that tells them apart; confusion is always between two things |
| `enforced-by` | the linter rule, validator, layer or procedure that holds it, or `not enforced` |
| `operations` | where it has them: fold and combine on a cluster, propagation on warrant |
| `example` | a pointer into the toy paper, below; never a corpus quotation |
| `references` | links to corpus instances and to the design note that argued for it; rendered when present, never required |
| `status` | `proposed` or `accepted`, with the ruling that accepted it |

A concept not in the canon is not a concept the system has. A term used in a prompt, a layer's
text, a skill file or the method that names no canon entry fails the check. This is how the
concepts stop living in three places at once.

## The toy paper

Examples come from one synthetic paper that lives in the machinery, about fifteen claims,
written to exercise every concept exactly once: a question with a hypothesis and one
alternative; a prediction; a finding with two results and a validating control; a scope
claim; an apparatus step; an observation; a synthesis; a tension; a qualifying caveat; one
loose claim with its reason. It is a fixture: every layer's tests run on it, the skill's primer
walks it end to end, and every canon example points into it. The machinery is then
self-teaching and self-checking with no corpus present, which is also what lets a new corpus
adopt it. Corpus instances become `references`: one link per concept to a real claim, cluster
or module on a site, shown when the corpus is there and skipped when it is not.

## Versioned, and named by what ran from it

The canon is a declaration, so it has the version every declaration has: a content digest
over its entries, computed the way `declaration_version` computes a layer's. Two things follow.

Every ledger entry records `canon: <version>` beside the layer's own declaration version, so
any run, and anything built from it, can say which definition of *warrant* or *module* it was
made under. When a canon entry changes, the layers whose prompts or code render that entry go
stale by the existing mechanism, because the contract they read changed; the canon version on
the ledger is what lets a reader see that a tree and its warrant were made under different
meanings of the word.

Canon entries are ruled on the way declarations are. An entry enters as `proposed` with its
design note as rationale, and `approve --declaration canon/<concept>` records the acceptance
in `runs/approvals.jsonl`. The kinds-of-decision note already says a scheme ruling is written
into the declaration; the canon is where that declaration now lives, and a scheme issue is a
question about a canon entry. The notes stay as history. The canon is what is current.

## Rendered, four times and as a page

From the one source, four surfaces render, and each is checked against it:

- **The contract**: what the model is sent. `contract.py` already renders relations and
  vocabulary; it renders every concept a prompt names.
- **The skill**: what an agent reads. `skill.py` already substitutes declared tokens into
  hand-written prose; the canon supplies the tokens for every concept, and the primer workflow
  walks the toy paper.
- **The method**: what a person reads. `docs/method.md`'s definitions become rendered
  sections, so the method cannot say something the machinery does not.
- **The schema**: what a consumer validates. The claim-set schema's closed value sets are
  generated from the canon's vocabularies, as `to_claim_set.py` half-does today.

And **the page**: `canon --render` writes one standalone HTML page from the canon and the toy
paper, in the register of the design pages built this week: a masthead with the canon version
and what changed since the last; one card per concept with its definition, its distinctions,
what enforces it, its toy example opened in place, and its references; the toy paper rendered
as modules with clusters folded; and, when a corpus is present, per concept the count of
instances and which runs were made under which canon version. The site includes it at
`/canon/`, and the machinery ships it without a corpus. It is the reference a person is sent
to, and the same bytes an agent is taught from.

## What checks it

`canon --check`, in `make check`: every entry has every field or says `not enforced`; every
term in the prompts, the layer texts, the skill and the method resolves to an entry; every
rendered surface is what the canon generates; the toy paper derives, under every layer, the
structure its fixture declares; and the committed page is the rendered one. The existing
contract and skill checks become cases of this one.

## What this does not decide

Whether the canon is YAML with prose or Python with data, as `relations.py` is today; either
renders, and the choice is which is easier to rule on in a PR. Whether corpus references are
curated by hand or found by a layer. Whether the page is also the place a ruling is recorded,
or only where it is shown.

## Cost

Moving the existing declarations under one roof and adding the concepts from the notes: one
day. The toy paper and the fixture tests: one day. The renderers, the check and the page: one
day. Ledger `canon:` and `approve --declaration canon/<concept>`: half a day. It follows the
modules layer (#53), whose test fixture is the toy paper's first draft.

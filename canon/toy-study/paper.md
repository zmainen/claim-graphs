# Toy study: does polishing a widget increase its shine?

*A synthetic fixture for the canon. Obviously fictional — no reader should mistake it for
research. It is the worked example every canon entry points into, carried through the four ways
a claim is made: composed (`design.yaml`), derived (`analysis/manifest.json`), induced (the
claim files under `claims/toy-widgets/`, from the prose below), and asserted about
(`review/verdict.json`).*

## Question and hypothesis

We asked whether polishing a widget increases its shine (**q1**). We hypothesised that polishing
raises shine (`hyp-polish-raises-shine`). A rival we set out to reject is that any handling, not
polishing specifically, is what raises shine (`alt-handling-raises-shine`). If polishing raises
shine, then polished widgets should read higher on the shine meter than unpolished widgets from
the same batch (`pred-polished-shinier`).

## Results

Polished widgets read higher on the shine meter than unpolished widgets (`finding-polished-shinier`;
fig1a): mean shine rose from 12 to 19 units (`result-shine-delta`), and the rise held in each of
three batches (`result-per-batch-shine`; fig1b). Widgets handled the same amount but left
unpolished showed no shine rise (`control-handled-no-rise`; fig2), ruling out that handling alone
is responsible. On the largest widgets the rise was smaller (`caveat-large-widgets-less`). An
independent gloss meter agreed on the average (`result-gloss-aggregate-agrees`; fig3), although
widget by widget the individual gloss scores did not track individual shine scores
(`result-gloss-individual-null`) — a tension the aggregate agreement does not resolve. Incidentally,
heavier widgets were duller regardless of polish (`obs-heavier-widgets-duller`; fig4), with weight
correlating negatively with shine (`obs-detail-weight-shine-corr`).

All widgets were brass, so the results are stated for brass only (`scope-brass-widgets`); the shine
meter was calibrated against a reference tile each session (`apparatus-shine-meter`).

## Interpretation

We read the effect as polishing abrading the dull surface oxide (`interp-polish-removes-oxide`).
Taken together, a widget's finish can be improved after manufacture (`loose-synth-widgets-improvable`).
